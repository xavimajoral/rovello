    How the index works, and what it cannot tell you

    The map covers the Anoia comarca only, in 0.75 km cells. Each cell gets six scores from 0 to 1, combined as a weighted geometric mean, so one failing factor pulls the whole index down.

    • Habitat. Where each species can occur, read from the iFong comarca maps of forest type, soil and species range. This carries the most weight: no mapped range means a very low score whatever the weather does.
    • Rain trigger. Rain that fell 7 to 16 days before the chosen week counts most, rain from the last 3 days not yet. Fruiting usually follows a soaking rain by one to two weeks.
    • Soil moisture. A simple water balance: rain in, evaporation out, a 100 mm store. North-facing slopes dry out more slowly.
    • Temperature. Mean of the last 10 days against the species' preferred range, with a penalty for forecast lows near freezing and for frost nights. A species far outside its range is also scaled down overall, which is why ou de reig scores low in November.
    • Elevation fit and slope aspect. A wide altitude band per species, and a small bonus for shaded north-facing ground.

    How the habitat layers were made. I read the iFong images for forest type, siliceous or calcareous soil and the range of each species, matched them to the comarca boundary and averaged them into cells. The totals agree with the shares iFong prints for the comarca (rovelló range 24% here against 25.6%, llenega negra 25% against 29.2%, ou de reig 1.2% against 1.3%), but this is a reading of pictures, not their dataset.

    Limits. Cep, camagroc, esclatassangs and trompeta de la mort are left out because iFong maps almost no range for them in Anoia. There is no habitat data outside the comarca, so the map stops at its boundary. Weather is sampled on a 10 km grid and terrain every 0.9 km, so local showers and small valleys are smoothed away. Private land, protected areas and picking limits are not in the data. The weights are rule-of-thumb and have not been checked against real finds.

    Before you pick. Identify everything with certainty before eating it, and check local picking rules: limits apply across Catalonia and some protected areas have their own rules.
